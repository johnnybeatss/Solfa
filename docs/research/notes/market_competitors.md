## 1. What plugins/apps already exist in the "healing/spiritual frequency" niche?

### Takeaway
The niche is crowded at the bottom and empty in the middle. It holds mostly **pitch/retuning utilities** (432/528 converters, from free to about $30), **free or old tone/binaural generators** (largely SaschArt), and a few **solfeggio pad synths**. I found **no parametric EQ marketed with solfeggio/432 presets** on KVR, Plugin Boutique or the other sources searched. A "spiritual-frequency EQ" would be a new category, but it competes with built-in DAW tuning and free retuners.

### Cited Findings

**Retuning / pitch-shift utilities (the closest existing category)**
- **Spiritual Tuner — Solfeggio Frequency Tuner (Spiritual Audio Labs)**: a pitch-shifter that converts 440 Hz to 432 Hz or to solfeggio frequencies. It has 10 solfeggio presets and fine/micro tuning, and is Windows VST3. The quoted discount price is $27.45 "until 30 Nov 2026", while the KVR marketplace shows a €14.90 intro price, so the sources conflict (medium-low confidence on price). — [KVR product](https://www.kvraudio.com/product/spiritual-tuner-solfeggio-frequency-tuner-by-spiritual-audio-labs); [KVR marketplace](https://www.kvraudio.com/marketplace/spiritual-tuner-solfeggio-frequency-tuner-by-spiritual-audio-labs)
- **RASTA 528Hz (Atix Universe)**: billed as a "mastering plugin" (VST/VST3). It retunes a project from 440 Hz to a "528 Hz-centered grid" while keeping tempo and tone, and can go on the master or on individual tracks. — [KVR](https://www.kvraudio.com/product/rasta-528hz-by-atix-universe)
- **432Hz MASTER (Atix Universe)**: the same developer's 432 product. Its marketing calls 440 Hz "blurry and unnatural" and 432 Hz "harmonic" and a "Universal Frequency of the cosmos". — [KVR product](https://www.kvraudio.com/product/432hz-master-by-atix-universe); [KVR marketplace](https://www.kvraudio.com/marketplace/432hz-master-by-atix-universe)
- **HertzBloom (touski)**: retunes from A4 = 440 Hz to any reference from 340 to 540 Hz with a claimed 0 ms latency. Formats are VST3 (Win/macOS) and AU (macOS). **Claims made by competitors**
- Most of the listings above make health or spiritual claims, the commonest being that 528 Hz "repairs DNA". The free/open-source reTuner is the clear exception: it is framed as pure tuning conversion. — [search summary of KVR/SaschArt listings](https://www.kvraudio.com/product/rasta-528hz-by-atix-universe); [Kushview](https://kushview.net/product/retuner/)

### Inferences
- **Gap: no EQ product exists in this niche.** Every existing product either retunes (pitch-shift) or generates tones. An EQ with "frequency presets" is a different mechanism from what buyers currently get. That is a positioning opportunity, but it also needs explaining: boosting 528 Hz with a bell does not make a track "in 528".
- **Quality gap:** competitors are mostly single-developer, Windows-only or legacy (32-bit, abandoned), with dated UI and heavy mystical copy. A polished, cross-platform (VST3/AU/AAX/CLAP) product with modern UX would stand out on craft alone.
- **Price anchor is low:** utilities sit at free to about $30, and a strong free open-source retuner (reTuner) now exists. A new product cannot charge for retuning alone. Value has to come from the EQ/tone-shaping and the workflow.
- A product could **bundle the three jobs** buyers currently assemble from separate tools: (a) global retune to A432 or C528, (b) a solfeggio drone/sine-bed generator, (c) EQ/tonal shaping. That combination does not appear to exist.

### Gaps
- Could not verify current prices, versions or user ratings directly on KVR (host blocked). KVR user reviews and star ratings for Spiritual Tuner, RASTA 528Hz and HertzBloom are **unknown**.
- Om Infinite Sounds Solfeggio price, and release dates for Spiritual Tuner and the Atix Universe products, were not captured.
- Did not search Splice/Loopmasters/ADSR for solfeggio sample packs specifically. A Kontakt library may exist and simply not have been indexed.
- Did not find any Reddit/Gearspace reception threads for these specific plugins.

## 2. How do producers actually use 432 Hz / solfeggio, and what are the demand signals?

### Takeaway
In practice, producers **retune** (via DAW master tune, synth tuning or cents offsets, or pitch-shifting finished audio) and layer **pure tones/pads** under the music. EQ-boosting a solfeggio frequency is not a documented mainstream technique. Demand is large and concentrated in YouTube/Spotify wellness content: the biggest channel has about 6.5M subscribers and 1.8B views, and one 528 Hz track has about 177M Spotify streams. These are snapshot figures; no trend line was found.

### Cited Findings

**Workflows**
- The standard method is to set the DAW's global tuning to A4 = 432 Hz. Ableton Live, Logic Pro and FL Studio allow this in project settings. — [Solfeggio Frequencies.org](https://solfeggiofrequencies.org/4-ways-to-re-tune-music-to-432hz-and-528hz/); [Mind University](https://mind.university/tools/432-hz)
- For "528 Hz", producers set A4 = 444 Hz so that C5 is about 528.01 Hz in equal temperament. The offsets are about +15.67 cents from A440 for C528 and about −31.77 cents for A432. Without a Hz control, producers use synth fine-tune in cents (one producer describes using Omnisphere's master tune). — [Anaamaly](https://anaamalymusic.com/page/tuning-music-432-hz-528-hz); [Toni Mazzotti](https://tonimazzotti.com/how-to-make-music-with-precise-temperament-tunings-for-432-hz-and-528-hz/); [Meditation Music Library](https://meditationmusiclibrary.com/blogs/wednesday-wisdom-blog/tune-music-solfeggio-frequencies)
- FL Studio's Master Pitch is "just a global offset" and does not recalculate the scale. Guides describe retuning the whole scale so that C5 lands on 528 Hz. — [Royalty Free Music Hub](https://royaltyfreemusichub.com/tune-your-daw-or-keyboard-to-solfeggio/)
- Pre-recorded audio is pitch-shifted, for example with Ableton's Shifter or a dedicated plugin. — [Solfeggio Frequencies.org](https://solfeggiofrequencies.org/4-ways-to-re-tune-music-to-432hz-and-528hz/); [YouTube: FL Studio 528/432 tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aeo5su0X9iw)
- KVR users suggest a tempo/pitch trick: record at 440, then slow it down so it lands at 432. Pitch-shifting by about 1.8% can introduce artifacts. — [KVR forum thread](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=8925865)
- Sound-bath and meditation content often layers tuning forks or pure tones with binaural "epsilon/delta" beats. — [Insight Timer example](https://insighttimer.com/thinkrootenergy/video-guided-meditations/deep-sleep-with-432-hz-528-hz-tuning-forks-epsilon-waves)
- "528 Hz" labelling is often musically confused because it mixes up key and tuning, and many tracks labelled 528 Hz are not verified. — [Frequency Generator Blog](https://www.frequencysoundgenerator.com/blog/healing-frequencies-432hz-528hz/); [Calm](https://www.calm.com/blog/528-hz)

**Demand signals (snapshots, not trends)**
- **Meditative Mind (YouTube)**: about 6.49M subscribers, about 1.8B views and 1,801 videos as of 2026-11-03 (vidIQ). HypeAuditor shows 6,485,048 for October 2026. It is described as the largest healing-frequency/528 Hz-focused channel. Income estimates vary widely (from about $2–3K to $26K per 30 days) and are modelled, not reported. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCM0YvsRfYfsniGAhjvYFOSA); [HypeAuditor](https://hypeauditor.com/youtube/UCM0YvsRfYfsniGAhjvYFOSA/); [Chillframe](https://chillframe.com/blog/meditation-music-youtube); [youtubers.me](https://us.youtubers.me/meditative-mind/youtube-estimated-earnings)
- **Spotify**: "528 Hz Love Frequency" by Miracle Tones (2019) has about 177.3M streams per a third-party counter. Other 528 Hz acts range from about 928 to about 54K monthly listeners. — [mystreamcount](https://www.mystreamcount.com/track/4ovQlgSLQsDpmE4SplH10J); [Spotify artist](https://open.spotify.com/artist/36aR0Fxxj6080e99GWxwRx); [Music Metrics Vault 528hz genre](https://www.musicmetricsvault.com/genres/528hz/2941)
- Spotify has a recognised "528hz" micro-genre, and curated playlists exist on Spotify and Amazon Music. — [Music Metrics Vault](https://www.musicmetricsvault.com/genres/528hz/2941); [Spotify playlist](https://open.spotify.com/playlist/0st31ulJLL716ii3zb46W3); [Amazon Music 432 Hz playlist](https://music.amazon.com/playlists/B0C1DSNV2G)
- Calm describes 528 Hz as having "gained popularity in digital wellness spaces" (qualitative only). — [Calm](https://www.calm.com/blog/528-hz)
- Splice describes 432 Hz as a "small but vocal minority" choice versus the 440 default. — [Splice blog](https://splice.com/blog/debunking-432-hz-tuning-standard)

### Inferences
- The core buyer is likely the **wellness content creator** (YouTube sound-bath, sleep, meditation, lo-fi/ambient channels) and the hobbyist producer who serves that audience, rather than mix engineers. Their need is "make my track legitimately 432/528 and show it", which is a **verification/labelling** problem as much as a sound problem.
- A feature that **proves tuning** (an on-screen readout like "A4 = 432.00 Hz, C5 = 528.0 Hz", or an exported tuning certificate or metadata tag) would answer a real pain point: "most tracks labelled 528 Hz are not verified."
- Built-in drone/sine-bed generation at exact solfeggio pitches, plus binaural offsets, matches how this content is actually made.
- An EQ alone does not match how producers use these frequencies. The EQ angle has to be framed as tonal emphasis or "resonance accents" layered on top of correct tuning.

- No Google Trends data could be retrieved. Year-over-year trend data for "432 Hz"/"528 Hz" searches, YouTube views and Spotify streams is **unverified**.
- No first-hand producer threads (Reddit r/WeAreTheMusicMakers, r/audioengineering, Gearspace) appeared in the index. Genre-specific usage (lo-fi vs ambient) is inferred, not sourced.
- No tutorial for "sine drone underneath" workflows was found, although sound-bath content suggests it is common.
- That Ableton Live 12 has native tuning systems/Scala support comes from training knowledge and was **not verified** this session.

## 3. How do leading EQs present tonal character and UX, and what is table stakes in 2026?

### Takeaway
Pro-Q 4 sets the 2026 baseline for a parametric EQ: about 24 bands, dynamic plus spectral-dynamic bands, EQ Match, Spectrum Grab, an instance list across tracks, character modes (Gentle/Warm), tilt shapes, variable slopes, a full-screen analyzer and immersive formats. Other "character" products sell one memorable move: Pultec boost/cut, Maag Air shelf, Baxandall smooth shelves. "Intelligent" EQs (soothe, Gullfoss, smart:EQ, Ozone) sell automation toward a target or profile.

### Cited Findings

**FabFilter Pro-Q 4 (reference product)**
- Headline additions over Pro-Q 3: **Spectral Dynamics** (a separate mode with a Spectral Density control), **EQ Sketch** (draw a curve and bands are created), the **Instance List** (view and control other instances, which one source says includes Pro-C 3/Pro-DS/Pro-G), **Character modes** (Gentle and Warm analog-style coloration), up to 24 bands, and variable slope from 12 to 96 dB/oct. — [Production Expert](https://www.production-expert.com/production-expert-1/fabfilter-pro-q-4-expert-review); [MixingGPT](https://mixinggpt.com/blog/fabfilter-pro-q-4-review-2026); [Sound on Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/fabfilter-pro-q-4?amp)
- The vendor listing highlights **Spectrum Grab, Full Screen mode and EQ Match**. EQ Match is accessed from the Instance List. Filter shapes include **Tilt Shelf and Flat Tilt**. — [Plugin Boutique listing](https://www.pluginboutique.com/products/14125); [Dubspot compare](https://blog.dubspot.com/plugins/compare/fabfilter-pro-q-4-vs-waves-puigtec)
- **Price:** $199 list on FabFilter's US shop (October 12, 2026, with a 30-day trial), with an $84 upgrade from Pro-Q 1/2/3. Plugin Boutique showed $149 (25% off) during a sale. Seasonal sales are typically about 25% off. — [LANDR/roundups via search](https://blog.landr.com/best-eq-plugins/); [Plugin Boutique](https://www.pluginboutique.com/product/2-Effects/16-EQ/14125-FabFilter-Pro-Q-4); [Production Expert](https://www.production-expert.com/production-expert-1/fabfilter-pro-q-4-expert-review)
- Immersive support is reported up to Dolby Atmos 9.1.6 by some sources and 7.1.2 by others (they conflict). — [Soundshaper](https://www.soundshaper.org/blog/fabfilter-pro-q-4-review-is-it-still-the-best-eq-plugin); [Stockmusicmusician](https://www.stockmusicmusician.com/blog/fab-filter-pro-q-3-review-tips)
- The main criticisms are price for basic users and the learning curve of spectral dynamics. — [Plugindrop](https://plugindrop.net/posts/fabfilter-pro-q-4-review/); [Sounds of Revolution](https://www.sounds-of-revolution.com/review-fabfilter-pro-q4/)

**Pultec-style (UAD, Waves PuigTec)**
- The "Pultec trick" is to boost and attenuate the low shelf at the same frequency setting. The two curves differ, which gives a tight low-end bump with a dip above it. The original manual advised against it, but it became commonplace. On a stock EQ it is emulated with a low shelf plus a bell. A high-frequency boost/attenuate equivalent exists for "air". — [Wikipedia Pultec EQP-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pultec_EQP-1); [Sounds Like You](https://soundslikeyoumusic.com/learn/pultec-low-end-trick); [Produce Like A Pro](https://producelikeapro.com/blog/pultec-eq/)
- The UAD Pultec Collection models the EQP-1A, MEQ-5 and HLF-3C. Third-party listings show UAD from $39+ and Waves PuigTec from $35+, and PuigTec is often heavily discounted. The search summary's claim that UAD "requires dedicated hardware" is **likely outdated**: UAD has offered native UADx versions since about 2022, though that is from training knowledge and was not verified here. — [Dubspot UAD vs PuigTec](https://blog.dubspot.com/plugins/compare/uad-pultec-collection-vs-waves-puigtec); [Equipboard](https://equipboard.com/items/waves-puigtec-eqs-plugin)

**Maag EQ4 Air Band**
- A boost-only high shelf with corners at 2.5, 5, 10, 20 and 40 kHz. The gentle slope (about 2 dB/oct) is credited with phase coherence and "airy" lift without harshness. Emulation tip: a stock high shelf at about 10.6 kHz, +3.5 dB. — [Maag EQ4-500 manual (PDF)](https://maag.audio/user_manual/EQ4_500_User_Manual.pdf); [Brainworx Maag EQ4 MS manual](https://files.plugin-alliance.com/products/maag_eq4_ms/maag_eq4_ms_manual.pdf); [Sage Audio](https://www.sageaudio.com/articles/recreating-analogs-most-elusive-secrets); [Sound on Sound EQ4M review](https://www.soundonsound.com/reviews/maag-audio-eq4m); [Milk Audio](https://www.milkaudiostore.com/intl/articles/recording-and-mix/maag-audio-and-the-air-band)

**Baxandall / tilt**
- Free BasiQ is a 3-band Baxandall-modelled EQ "known for smooth shelves and neutral response". Pro-Q 4 includes Tilt Shelf/Flat Tilt shapes. No dedicated tilt-EQ product was captured. — [search summary citing MusicRadar EQ guide](https://www.musicradar.com/how-to/how-to-choose-the-right-eq-plugin-for-the-job); [Plugin Boutique Pro-Q 4](https://www.pluginboutique.com/products/14125)

**Resonance suppression / intelligent EQ**
- **soothe (oeksound)**: soothe2 now redirects to **soothe3** at about $199 list ($139–159 on sale, per a May 2026 table). It is cut-only resonance suppression. — [Dubspot soothe2](https://blog.dubspot.com/plugins/soothe2); [MixingGPT soothe 3 review](https://mixinggpt.com/blog/oeksound-soothe-3-review-2026)
- **Gullfoss (Soundtheory)**: hundreds of continuous program-dependent boosts and cuts to reshape tonal balance, at about €178 to $199. — [Dubspot Gullfoss vs soothe2](https://blog.dubspot.com/plugins/compare/gullfoss-vs-soothe2); [Producer School](https://theproducerschool.com/blogs/featured-blogs/soothe2-vs-gullfoss-which-smart-eq-plugin-is-right-for-you)
- **Sonible smart:EQ 4**: about €89 / $89. It has a smart:filter that balances toward a chosen **Profile**, Track/Group/Track+Group modes, and **cross-channel unmasking** across a front/middle/background hierarchy (up to 10 tracks). Group View controls other instances remotely. User comments are mixed ("assistant features do too much"). — [sonible smart:EQ 4](https://www.sonible.com/smarteq4/); [sonible blog](https://www.sonible.com/blog/everything-new-smarteq4/); [Gearspace thread](https://gearspace.com/threads/sonible-smart-eq-4-intelligent-equalizer.1419937/page-11)
- **iZotope Ozone 12**: the Master Assistant is in every edition and is "more steerable" (set goals and genre). **Tonal Balance Control 2** compares the spectrum against genre or custom targets and is bundled with Advanced (about $399–499). Ozone's Stabilizer is described as iZotope's take on Gullfoss, trained on in-house targets. — [iZotope Ozone 12 Advanced](https://www.izotope.com/products/ozone-advanced); [Stickz review](https://stickz.co/blog/ozone-12-worth-the-hype-our-review/); [Lord Reverb](https://lordreverb.com/plugins/izotope-ozone-12-advanced-review/); [Gearspace Ozone 11 vs Gullfoss/soothe](https://gearspace.com/board/mastering-forum/1415651-ozone-11-spectral-plugins-vs-gullfoss-soothe-2-a.html)
- **TDR Nova (Tokyo Dawn Labs)**: **free** parallel dynamic EQ with 4 bands, each with full dynamics, plus HP/LP filters and wideband compression. The GE version is about €60 with 6 bands. It is widely recommended as the best free dynamic EQ. — [TDR Nova](https://www.tokyodawn.net/tdr-nova/free/); [BPB free dynamic EQ roundup](https://bedroomproducersblog.com/free-vst-plugins/dynamic-eq/); [MusicRadar](https://www.musicradar.com/music-tech/plugins/fantastic-free-plugins-and-how-to-use-them-tdr-nova)

### Inferences
- **Table stakes for a 2026 parametric EQ** (inferred from Pro-Q 4 as the benchmark and a free TDR Nova as the floor):
  - a real-time analyzer with Spectrum Grab or click-to-band
  - at least 8 bands with bell/shelf/HP/LP/notch/tilt shapes and variable slope
  - dynamic EQ per band
  - M/S or stereo placement
  - linear-phase/natural-phase options
  - resizable full-screen GPU UI
  - A/B states, undo/redo and preset browser
  - sidechain
  - VST3/AU/AAX (CLAP increasingly)
  - a free trial
- EQ Match, an instance list, spectral dynamics and AI/profile assist are differentiators, not table stakes.
- **Borrowable UX ideas:**
  - Maag-style **one-knob "named" band**: a boost-only gentle shelf with stepped corners. This maps cleanly onto stepped solfeggio frequency corners (174/285/396/417/528/639/741/852/963 Hz).
  - Pultec-style **paired boost/cut at one frequency**.
  - smart:EQ/Ozone-style **target profiles**: a "solfeggio/432 tonal profile" target curve.
  - Pro-Q **EQ Sketch** and **instance list**: a session-wide view showing every track's tuning reference and frequency emphasis.
  - soothe-style **resonance suppression** at non-target frequencies, which reframes "harmonizing to 528" as attenuating clashing resonances.
- A **frequency-snap** feature, where band centres snap to solfeggio frequencies or to the 432-tuned note grid (the musical-note EQ snapping some EQs offer), is a natural, credible "spiritual" UX hook that is also musically defensible: bands land on in-tune pitches.

### Gaps
- Could not directly verify Pro-Q 4's release date (training knowledge says December 2024), its official feature list, or the immersive channel-count conflict.
- Exact current UAD/Waves Pultec prices and UADx native availability were not verified this session.
- No verified source on a standalone tilt-EQ product (for example a Tonelux Tilt emulation), so that comparison is thin.
- No independent reviews (Sound on Sound/MusicTech) of smart:EQ 4 or Ozone 12 were fully read. Feature claims come largely from vendor pages and review blogs.

## 4. Pricing norms for indie EQ plugins in 2026 and distribution channels

### Takeaway
Indie EQs cluster at **$29–$149**, under the $199 premium anchor (Pro-Q 4, soothe3, Gullfoss) and above a strong free floor (TDR Nova, reTuner). Heavy discounting is endemic: 49% of indie developers say prices are falling. ## 5. Criticism and backlash toward "spiritual frequency" products in audio communities

### Takeaway
Audio communities overwhelmingly treat 432 Hz as a legitimate but arbitrary reference pitch, and treat the healing, cosmic or DNA claims as pseudoscience or "snake oil". Backlash targets the **marketing claims**, not the tool. Neutrally framed tools like Kushview reTuner draw no such criticism. A product making health claims also invites regulatory and credibility risk.

### Cited Findings
- KVR users: "I don't believe in this frequency magic nonsense". "There's no conspiracy against 432hz... There's just no point to retuning every instrument to 432, it doesn't make any difference". Healing-claim threads get pushed to an off-topic area ("Hyde Park Corner"). One thread argues 431.9 vs 432 Hz is inaudible (just-noticeable-difference). — [KVR "best tuning plugin from 440"](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=8925865); [KVR thread p2](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=8927304); [KVR 432 in Auto-Tune](https://kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=8318375); [KVR "440 to 432 sounding more natural?"](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=5800312)
- **Splice** "debunking" article: tune to whatever concert pitch sounds best. Proponents' claims about ancient instruments being tuned to 432 have little evidence, and the cymatics "proof" depends on the surface and frequency. — [Splice](https://splice.com/blog/debunking-432-hz-tuning-standard)
- **Adam Neely (2019 video):** most 432 justifications only "make numerical sense" in "wacky tuning systems". This is a widely seen YouTube critique in producer culture. — [TheTVDB episode listing](https://www.thetvdb.com/series/adam-neely/episodes/8712656)
- **Origins:** the 432 Hz push is traced to LaRouche-linked organisations (Schiller Institute "Verdi tuning"). There is no scientific evidence of healing benefits. — [Journal of Popular Music Studies](https://online.ucpress.edu/jpms/article-abstract/33/1/137/116319); [Wikipedia: Scientific pitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_pitch)
- **Solfeggio origins:** the frequencies were popularised from a 1970s book that derived them numerologically from a Latin hymn. Proponents disagree on the key frequencies. There is no peer-reviewed clinical evidence that 528 Hz repairs DNA. — [Frequency Generator Blog](https://www.frequencysoundgenerator.com/blog/healing-frequencies-432hz-528hz/); [Skeptoid #474](https://skeptoid.com/episodes/474); [HowStuffWorks](https://science.howstuffworks.com/science-vs-myth/unexplained-phenomena/solfeggio-frequencies.htm); [Glasgow Guardian "making a quick buck"](https://glasgowguardian.co.uk/2023/03/29/solfeggio-frequencies-and-making-a-quick-buck/)
- **Evidence that does exist (weak):** a small, non-randomised 2019 study found slight heart-rate and blood-pressure decreases with 432 Hz. A randomised crossover trial in cancer patients found a more pronounced heart-rate effect for 432 vs 443 Hz, but its authors call for larger RCTs. — [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/can-tuning-music-to-432hz-really-heal-you-scientists-explain-the-viral-trend/); [PMC11755923](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11755923/)
- Even sound-healing practitioners' sites concede "claims vs science" gaps. — [Sound Medicine Academy](https://www.soundmedicineacademy.com/pages/sound-healing-blog/solfeggio-frequencies)
- Calm, a mainstream wellness brand, says 528 Hz health claims are contested. — [Calm](https://www.calm.com/blog/528-hz)

### Inferences
- **Positioning implication:** sell the product as a precise **creative and tuning tool for wellness/meditation creators**. Use accurate, non-medical copy ("tuned to A=432 / C=528 references", "inspired by the solfeggio tradition") and avoid "heals", "repairs DNA" or "cosmic". That keeps both audiences: wellness creators get the presets and labels, and skeptical producers get a well-built EQ they won't mock. reTuner's neutral framing shows this is viable.
- Anticipate the "key vs tuning" critique: a bell boost at 528 Hz does not make a track "528 Hz music". Documentation should explain what each preset actually does (for example, "emphasises the 528 Hz region / C5 at A=444 tuning").
- An optional "about the science" page or transparent info panel could turn the critique into trust.
- Making health claims for software may draw consumer-protection scrutiny (for example FTC/ASA rules on health claims). This is an inference and was not researched here.

### Gaps
- No Reddit (r/WeAreTheMusicMakers, r/audioengineering) or Gearspace threads were indexed, so the volume and tone of community backlash there is **unverified** (expected to resemble KVR, but unconfirmed).
- No reviews from Sound on Sound, MusicRadar or MusicTech of any spiritual-frequency plugin were found. The category appears to get no mainstream press coverage.
- No specific regulatory actions against audio "healing frequency" products were researched.

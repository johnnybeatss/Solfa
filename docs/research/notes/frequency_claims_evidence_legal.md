# "Spiritual / Healing Frequency" Claims in Music: Origins, Evidence, and Legal Limits on Marketing

Research date: 2026-11-21. Method note: this session's network blocked direct page fetches of PubMed, SCIRP and similar hosts, so findings come from web-search result summaries of the cited pages (FTC, PMC, ASA, Skeptoid, etc.). Exact figures that could not be checked against the full text are flagged "unverified". Confidence levels: **High** = several independent reliable sources agree, or a primary/official source; **Medium** = one reliable source or consistent secondary reporting; **Low** = thin, conflicting or only from proponents.

Labels used: **[VERIFIED]** = documented fact. **[PROPONENT CLAIM]** = what promoters assert, reported only so the claim can be identified.

## 1. Solfeggio frequencies (174, 285, 396, 417, 528, 639, 741, 852, 963 Hz): origins and relation to Guido d'Arezzo

### Takeaway
The "Solfeggio frequencies" are a modern invention. They come from Joseph Puleo's numerology on verse numbers in the Book of Numbers and were popularised by Leonard Horowitz's 1999 book *Healing Codes for the Biological Apocalypse*. Apart from the name, they have no historical link to Guido d'Arezzo's medieval solmization, which named relative scale steps, not absolute frequencies in Hz. The original set had six tones. 174, 285 and 963 Hz were added later, and no documented rationale for them was found. (Confidence: **High** that this is modern and unrelated to Guido. **Medium** on the exact details of how Puleo and Horowitz derived the numbers.)

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED] The origin story appears in Leonard Horowitz's *Healing Codes for the Biological Apocalypse*. In Horowitz's telling, Joseph Puleo worked out that the frequencies were embedded in the Hymn to St John the Baptist (*Ut queant laxis*, attributed to Paul the Deacon, c. 720–799), supposedly hidden there long before. Critics question how Puleo "found" the tones. (High) — [Skeptoid #474](https://skeptoid.com/episodes/474); [Brendan Murphy Substack (proponent-leaning)](https://officialbrendanmurphy.substack.com/p/the-mysterious-solfeggio-tones)
- [VERIFIED] Accounts of Puleo's method disagree. One says the numbers came from "a pattern of verse numbers in the Bible's Book of Numbers" (1970s). Another says Puleo found "six repeating codes" around the "sacred numbers 3, 6, and 9". A third says Puleo "was allegedly given the secret... in a vision". (Medium; the sources conflict) — [Discovery UK](https://www.discoveryuk.com/?p=23456027); [Meer](https://meer.com/en/75006-solfeggio-frequencies); [Glasgow Guardian](https://glasgowguardian.co.uk/2023/03/29/solfeggio-frequencies-and-making-a-quick-buck/)
- [VERIFIED] The 1999 book credits Puleo with six recurring numbers (396, 417, 528, 639, 741, 852), found by applying Pythagorean (digit) reduction to verses in the Book of Numbers. A seller's brochure instead dates Puleo's "rediscovery" to 1974. (Medium) — [OmConscious guide](https://omconscious.pages.dev/guides/solfeggio-frequencies-what-the-science-shows); [Solfeggio tuning-fork brochure (seller)](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0288/6804/files/Solfeggio_Tuning_Forks_brochure.pdf)
- [VERIFIED] 174, 285 and 963 Hz were later additions to the original six, making the nine-tone set now in common use. No source gives a principled origin for them. One source reports no direct research at all on 285 Hz. (Medium on the "later addition" point; the sources are mostly wellness sites) — [OmConscious guide](https://omconscious.pages.dev/guides/solfeggio-frequencies-what-the-science-shows); [Medium post (proponent)](https://medium.com/@greatlife397.line/the-solfeggio-frequencies-and-the-benefits-behind-them-9356d467daeb)
- [VERIFIED] Guido d'Arezzo used the hymn *Ut queant laxis* to name the solmization syllables. "Other than the name, the two concepts are unrelated." (High) — [Skeptoid #474](https://skeptoid.com/episodes/474); [Discovery UK](https://www.discoveryuk.com/?p=23456027)
- [VERIFIED] Guido's system assigned syllables to intervals, not to exact Hz values, so specific Hz numbers cannot trace back to that tradition. (High) — [OmConscious guide](https://omconscious.pages.dev/guides/solfeggio-frequencies-what-the-science-shows)
- [VERIFIED] Played as listed on most websites, the "Solfeggio scale" sounds slightly out of tune. (Medium) — [Skeptoid #474](https://skeptoid.com/episodes/474)
- [VERIFIED] Skeptical and fact-checking reviews find no empirical evidence or reasoning behind the claimed functions. Science Feedback specifically rated "solfeggio frequencies remove toxins from the body" as unsupported. Interest remains "anecdotal, relying on personal testimonies". (High) — [Science Feedback](https://science.feedback.org/review/no-evidence-support-claim-solfeggio-frequencies-remove-toxins-from-body/); [Discovery UK](https://www.discoveryuk.com/?p=23456027)
- [PROPONENT CLAIM] Each tone has a function, for example 528 Hz "Mi" for "DNA repair" or "transformation". These per-frequency assignments trace to Horowitz-era promotional material. (Claim is documented; there is no evidence for it) — [Women of Grace critique, "Can Special Sound Frequencies Heal Your DNA?"](https://womenofgrace.com/blog/75568); [Science Feedback](https://science.feedback.org/review/no-evidence-support-claim-solfeggio-frequencies-remove-toxins-from-body/)
- A 2025-era news report on a small 528 Hz study quotes researchers warning against claims that a frequency "boosts the brain" or "heals anything". (Medium) — [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/listening-to-specific-music-frequencies-alters-stress-markers-in-students-facing-exams/)

### Inferences
- Background knowledge, not checked in this session: in *Ut queant laxis* each half-line starts one step higher (C-D-E-F-G-A, the hexachord). This is a relative-pitch teaching device from about 1025 CE. There was no absolute pitch standard in the 11th century; European pitch varied widely until the 19th–20th centuries (see Section 3), so a medieval "528 Hz" is impossible.
- All the core numbers reduce to digital roots of 3, 6 or 9 (for example 3+9+6=18 → 9; 5+2+8=15 → 6). This fits the numerological "3-6-9" story above and is arithmetic, not acoustics.
- For a plugin, it is accurate to describe the presets as "based on the popular modern 'Solfeggio' number set (popularised in 1999)". Calling them "ancient Gregorian frequencies" is historically false.

### Gaps
- I could not access the primary text of Horowitz & Puleo (1999) to quote the exact derivation, or to confirm who first added 174, 285 and 963 Hz and when. Wellness sources repeat the "added later" point without naming who added them.

## 2. Peer-reviewed studies on 528 Hz

### Takeaway
The main human study (Akimoto et al., 2018) had only 9 participants in a single-session crossover, appeared in a low-tier open-access journal, and compared 528 Hz against one 440 Hz control. It reported short-term drops in salivary cortisol and rises in oxytocin. It cannot support healing, anxiety-treatment or DNA claims, and the authors themselves say claimed solfeggio effects lack scientific basis. Animal and cell studies use non-musical conditions (for example 100 dB in rats). (Confidence: **High** that the evidence is weak and preliminary.)

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED] Akimoto K, et al. (Juntendo University), "Effect of 528 Hz Music on the Endocrine System and Autonomic Nervous System", *Health* (Scientific Research Publishing / SCIRP), 2018. (High) — [SCIRP paper page](https://www.scirp.org/journal/PaperInformation.aspx?paperID=87146); [full text HTML](https://file.scirp.org/Html/2-8204397_87146.htm)
- [VERIFIED] Design: 9 healthy adults (1 man, 8 women, aged 26–37). Each heard the same piano music tuned to 528 Hz and to 440 Hz on separate days. Measures: salivary cortisol, chromogranin A, oxytocin, plus mood scales (POMS). (High) — [SCIRP](https://www.scirp.org/journal/PaperInformation.aspx?paperID=87146); [PsyPost summary](https://www.psypost.org/listening-to-specific-music-frequencies-alters-stress-markers-in-students-facing-exams/)
- [VERIFIED] Results: in the 528 Hz condition, cortisol fell significantly, chromogranin A tended to fall, and oxytocin rose significantly. Tension-anxiety and Total Mood Disturbance scores dropped. No significant changes occurred at 440 Hz. Popular summaries stress an effect "even following only five minutes of exposure". (High that these are the reported results) — [SCIRP](https://file.scirp.org/Html/2-8204397_87146.htm); [ScienceInsights](https://scienceinsights.org/what-is-528-hz-good-for-benefits-and-claims-explained/)
- [VERIFIED] The authors write that various effects have been ascribed to the solfeggio frequency, "but none of these have any scientific basis". (Medium; quoted through a search summary of the paper) — [SCIRP](https://www.scirp.org/journal/PaperInformation.aspx?paperID=87146)
- [VERIFIED] A 2019 rat study in *Genes & Genomics* reported that 528 Hz sound at 100 dB increased testosterone production in the brain. This is animal work at a very loud intensity and is not comparable to music listening. (Medium) — [ScienceInsights summary](https://scienceinsights.org/what-is-528-hz-good-for-benefits-and-claims-explained/); [PubMed 30414050](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30414050/)
- [VERIFIED] A 2025-era study in *Brain and Behavior* on exam-stressed students reported lower levels of a cellular stress marker in a 528 Hz group than in control and 432 Hz groups. The researchers cautioned against "boosts the brain / heals" claims. (Medium; read only through press coverage) — [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/listening-to-specific-music-frequencies-alters-stress-markers-in-students-facing-exams/)

### Inferences
- Limitations of Akimoto 2018, from the reported design: n=9 with 8 of 9 women, so it is badly underpowered and not representative. Blinding and randomisation of session order are not described in the summaries. There was a single short exposure. It compared within-condition change scores and did not test 528 against 440 directly, which is a common statistical error ("difference in significance is not a significant difference"). It reported many outcomes with no correction mentioned. It has not been replicated. SCIRP has a long-standing reputation as a low-rigour or predatory-adjacent publisher (background knowledge, not checked here).
- "528 Hz music" usually means the whole piece is retuned so a reference note sits at 528 Hz. That is a global pitch shift of about +31 cents relative to C at A=440 (C5 = 523.25 Hz). It is not an EQ boost at 528 Hz. An EQ preset boosting 528 Hz is a different stimulus from anything studied.
- No credible human study supports "DNA repair" at 528 Hz. The DNA claim traces to Horowitz-era marketing, not to peer-reviewed evidence.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the DOI, volume and pages of Akimoto 2018, or confirm the session-order randomisation and blinding, from the full text. I could not fetch the *Brain and Behavior* paper to get its sample size. I found no peer-reviewed in-vitro "528 Hz repairs DNA" study in any reputable journal; if one exists it was not found in this search.

## 3. 432 Hz tuning: history, the "Nazi 440" myth, and the studies

### Takeaway
Concert pitch varied for centuries. Verdi favoured a lower pitch, and Italy adopted A=432 in 1881. A 1939 international conference in London recommended A=440. ISO adopted it as Recommendation R 16 in 1955 and as ISO 16 in 1975. The claim that Goebbels or the Nazis imposed 440 to manipulate people has no contemporary documentary support. It traces to advocacy writing (Schiller Institute/LaRouche circles, a 1988 article), and historians dismiss it. The 432 vs 440 studies are small pilots, mostly from one research group, with mixed controls. Two dental RCTs found music helped anxiety with little difference between tunings. The tuning difference is only about 31.8 cents, smaller than a quarter-tone. (Confidence: **High** on the standardisation history and on the Nazi claim lacking support. **High** that the 432 health evidence is weak.)

### Cited Findings
**History**
- [VERIFIED] In May 1939, delegates from France, Germany, the Netherlands, Italy and England met at the BBC's Broadcasting House in London and recommended A=440 Hz. *Nature* reported on the meeting. (High) — [Wikipedia: A440](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A440_(pitch_standard)); [Red Bull Music Academy](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2017/10/a440-tuning-standard-feature); [Gribenski 2020, Max Planck (scholarly history)](https://pure.mpg.de/pubman/item/item_3195091_10/component/file_3345554/gribenski_2020_plenty.pdf)
- [VERIFIED] ISO adopted the recommendation in 1955 as Recommendation R 16 and formalised it in 1975 as ISO 16. (High) — [Wikipedia: A440](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A440_(pitch_standard))
- [VERIFIED] Italian musicians adopted A=432 as a standard in 1881 on Verdi's advice. The modern "Verdi tuning" campaign was promoted by the LaRouche movement's Schiller Institute. (High) — [Wikipedia: Concert pitch](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Kammerton); [Jakub Marian](https://jakubmarian.com/the-432-hz-vs-440-hz-conspiracy-theory/)
- [VERIFIED] The Nazi/Goebbels link traces to an article published in 1988, and that article does not say the frequency was used to manipulate enemies. Historian of Nazi propaganda Jeffrey Herf dismissed the idea that the Nazis used A=440 to manipulate people. No contemporary source supports the claim, which appears mainly in 432 advocacy material. (High) — [Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/international/did-nazis-change-the-universal-music-pitch-to-use-against-their-enemies-675733); [Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2021/01/27/how-a-centuries-old-debate-about-musical-tuning-turned-into-a-nazi-conspiracy-theory.html/amp); [Wikipedia Talk: Concert pitch](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk:Concert_pitch)

**Studies**
- [VERIFIED] Calamassi D, Pomponi GP. "Music Tuned to 440 Hz Versus 432 Hz and the Health Effects: A Double-blind Cross-over Pilot Study." *Explore* (2019), doi:10.1016/j.explore.2019.04.001. 33 participants. It reported a slight decrease in heart rate with 432 Hz. One secondary source puts the drop at an average of 4.79 bpm versus 440 Hz (**unverified** against the paper). Reviewers call the study underpowered. (Medium) — [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/can-tuning-music-to-432hz-really-heal-you-scientists-explain-the-viral-trend/); [ScienceInsights](https://scienceinsights.org/what-does-432-hz-do-to-your-brain-and-body/); [Slow Wave Labs review](https://slowwavelabs.pages.dev/learn/is-432-hz-scientifically-proven); [ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/432hz-brain-tuning-is-an-ancient-idea-does-it-actually-work)
- [VERIFIED] Calamassi et al. 2020 (sleep): double-blind crossover pilot with 12 spinal-cord-injury patients given their favourite music at 440 or 432 Hz. Sleep scores improved at 432 (+3.6, p=0.02) but not at 440 (−1.50, p=0.34). The authors call for larger samples. (Medium) — [Acta Biomedica (Mattioli)](https://mattioli1885journals.com/index.php/actabiomedica/article/view/10755); [FLORE, Univ. Florence](https://flore.unifi.it/handle/2158/1458112)
- [VERIFIED] Calamassi et al. 2022: randomised, double-blind pilot in emergency nurses during COVID-19 comparing 440 Hz music, 432 Hz music and a free activity, measuring state anxiety and stress. The authors say 432 Hz "may help" and that further studies are needed. (Medium; the PMC ID mapping is from a search summary) — [PMC9534204](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9534204)
- [VERIFIED] Aravena et al. 2020, *J Appl Oral Sci*: RCT with n=42 tooth-extraction patients randomised to 432 Hz, 440 Hz or no music. Both music groups had lower anxiety than controls. The 432 group had lower salivary cortisol. (High) — [PMC7213780](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7213780/)
- [VERIFIED] Menziletoglu et al. 2020 (*Med Oral Patol Oral Cir Bucal*): 432 Hz music vs binaural beats before third-molar surgery. Both reduced dental anxiety. (Medium) — [PMC7806348](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7806348/)
- [VERIFIED] A systematic review of 432 Hz research concluded that further research is needed to evaluate effectiveness. A 2019 film-soundtrack heart-rate study was limited by a very small, non-randomised sample. (Medium) — [PMC8023109](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8023109); [ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/432hz-brain-tuning-is-an-ancient-idea-does-it-actually-work)
- [VERIFIED] Reviewers conclude there is no rigorous evidence that 432 Hz music is meaningfully better than 440 Hz for relaxation, sleep or health. (Medium) — [Slow Wave Labs](https://slowwavelabs.pages.dev/learn/is-432-hz-scientifically-proven); [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/can-tuning-music-to-432hz-really-heal-you-scientists-explain-the-viral-trend/)

**Can listeners tell 432 from 440?**
- [VERIFIED] In Hedger, Heald & Nusbaum (2013, *Psychological Science*), music slowly detuned by 33 cents during a piece went undetected by listeners, including people with absolute pitch, whose "in tune" categories then shifted. (High) — [APS press release](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/perfect-pitch-may-not-be-absolute-after-all.html); [Hedger et al. PDF](https://apexlab.uchicago.edu/docs/papers/AbsolutePitch.pdf)
- [VERIFIED] Van Hedger & Bongiovanni (2023, *Music & Science*): ordinary listeners judged whether isolated notes and chords matched A440 tuning above chance, but the ability weakened with more musical context. (Medium) — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/5025f5016cfa47a492a96a174118f2b5)
- [VERIFIED] Van Hedger et al. (2023): liking of A440 vs about 50-cent-detuned excerpts depends on familiarity with the music. (Medium) — [Van Hedger 2023 PACA PDF](https://svanhedger.github.io/pubs/2023_PACA.pdf)

### Inferences
- 1200 × log2(440/432) = 31.8 cents (computed here). This is less than a third of a semitone and about the same as the 33-cent drift that went unnoticed in Hedger 2013. In a blind A/B, listeners can probably tell two isolated reference tones apart, but they generally cannot name which tuning a full piece of music is in.
- Most 432 studies come from one group (Calamassi, Florence), are pilots (n=12–33), and compare within-condition change rather than testing the difference between conditions. Where a no-music control exists (Aravena), music itself carries most of the anxiety effect. Note also that a "432 Hz" track is a whole-mix pitch shift; an EQ cannot change tuning.
- Background knowledge, not checked in this session: France's 1859 "diapason normal" was A=435, and pre-1939 pitch ranged widely (roughly A=415–460).

### Gaps
- I could not confirm from full text the exact Calamassi & Pomponi 2019 outcomes (blood pressure, respiratory rate, satisfaction) or whether the effect sizes were statistically different between conditions. I found no study that directly tests 432 vs 440 discrimination in real music.

## 4. Schumann resonance (7.83 Hz)

### Takeaway
Schumann resonances are real. They are extremely-low-frequency *electromagnetic* standing waves in the cavity between Earth and the ionosphere, excited by lightning. The fundamental is about 7.8 Hz, with modes near 14, 20, 27 and 34 Hz, and the values vary with time of day and ionospheric conditions. They are not sound. Claims that they are Earth's "heartbeat", that people need to be "in tune" with them, or that a 7.83 Hz audio tone brings health benefits are pseudoscience. (Confidence: **High**.)

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED] Lightning excites standing waves in the Earth–ionosphere cavity. The fundamental mode has a wavelength equal to Earth's circumference. (High) — [Wikipedia: Schumann resonances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schumann_resonances); [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/starts-with-a-bang/entire-earth-resonates/)
- [VERIFIED] The ideal calculation gives about 7.5 Hz. Finite ionospheric conductivity lowers propagation speed, and the real cavity is imperfect. Higher modes sit near 14, 20, 27 and 34 Hz. Values vary daily with global lightning activity and with ionospheric and geomagnetic conditions. (High) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schumann_resonances); [Earth–ionosphere waveguide](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth-ionosphere_waveguide)
- [VERIFIED] "An entire pseudoscience industry has sprung up around Schumann resonances online." Big Think: "Amazing physics, sham medicine." (High) — [BBC Sky at Night](https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/space-science/schumann-resonances); [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/?p=454830)
- [PROPONENT CLAIM] 7.83 Hz is "Earth's heartbeat" and matches human alpha/theta brain rhythms, so playing it heals or "grounds" people. (No support in the sources above.)

### Inferences
- 7.83 Hz is well below the usual lower limit of human hearing (about 20 Hz; background knowledge). It also sits below the useful range of most speakers and headphones. An EQ band at 7.83 Hz only affects subsonic content, which DC/sub filters usually remove. It cannot "add" the Schumann resonance. A plugin could only reference 7.83 Hz as a *modulation rate* (for example LFO or tremolo speed) or a binaural/isochronic beat rate, and should describe it as a thematic reference, not a functional one.

### Gaps
- I did not find peer-reviewed human studies specifically testing *audio* at 7.83 Hz. None surfaced.

## 5. Binaural beats and isochronic tones

### Takeaway
Binaural beats are a real auditory illusion generated in the brainstem when slightly different tones reach each ear. They require headphones (one tone per ear) and work best with carriers below about 1000 Hz, with an upper limit some report near 1500 Hz. Beat rates above roughly 30–40 Hz are heard as roughness or separate tones. One meta-analysis (Garcia-Argibay et al., 2019; 22 studies) found a medium overall effect (g=0.45) on cognition, anxiety and pain, but with high heterogeneity. The 2023 systematic review (Ingendoh et al.) found that most EEG studies did *not* support the "brainwave entrainment" mechanism (5 for, 8 against, 1 mixed). Evidence for isochronic tones is even thinner. (Confidence: **High** on the mechanism and its limits. **Medium** that there are modest anxiety effects. **High** that the entrainment mechanism is unproven.)

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED] Garcia-Argibay M, Santed MA, Reales JM. "Efficacy of binaural auditory beats in cognition, anxiety, and pain perception: a meta-analysis." *Psychol Res* 2019;83(2):357–372, doi:10.1007/s00426-018-1066-8 (epub Aug 2018). 22 studies, 35 effect sizes, overall g=0.45 (medium). Masking with white or pink noise made no difference. Exposure before, or before and during, the task worked better than exposure only during it. Effect direction and size depended on beat frequency, exposure time and timing. (High) — [PubMed 30073406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30073406/); [Univ. Southampton eprint](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/488754)
- [VERIFIED] Ingendoh RM, Posny ES, Heine A. "Binaural beats to entrain the brain? A systematic review of the effects of binaural beat stimulation on brain oscillatory activity, and the implications for psychological research and intervention." *PLoS ONE* 2023 (19 May), doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0286023. Of 14 studies, 5 supported entrainment, 8 contradicted it and 1 was mixed. The studies were "very heterogeneous" in stimulation, design and EEG analysis, and the authors call for standardisation. (High) — [PMC10198548](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10198548); [PubMed 37205669](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37205669/)
- [VERIFIED] A 2023 meta-analysis on memory and attention reported mixed results and called for more robust designs. A preregistered study found strong evidence *against* beta-frequency binaural beats improving sustained attention. (Medium) — [PubMed 35842538](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35842538/); [PubMed 33978805](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33978805/)
- [VERIFIED] A systematic review of binaural beats and acute and chronic pain exists. (Medium; its conclusion was not retrieved) — [PMC10785528](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10785528/)
- [VERIFIED] In a dental-anxiety trial, binaural beats and 432 Hz music both reduced preoperative anxiety. (Medium) — [PMC7806348](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7806348/)
- [VERIFIED] Cedars-Sinai summarises the evidence on binaural-beat entrainment as contradictory and says there is no strong evidence for broad healing claims. (Medium) — [Cedars-Sinai](https://www.cedars-sinai.org/stories-and-insights/healthy-living/sound-advice-do-sound-therapy-and-binaural-beats-work)
- [VERIFIED] Mechanism limits. Oster (1973, *Scientific American*) found binaural beats are heard best with carriers around 440 Hz, become less distinct above that, and vanish above about 1000 Hz (other investigators report up to about 1500 Hz), confirming earlier work by Licklider. Below about 90 Hz, listeners may confuse the beats with the tones. Rapid beats up to about 30 Hz are heard as roughness; beyond that the two tones are heard separately. Another source puts the binaural limit near 40 Hz difference. (High on the carrier limit. Medium on the exact beat-rate ceiling.) — [PMC4428073 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4428073); [PMC11367212](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11367212/); [Wikipedia: Beat (acoustics)/Binaural beats](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binaural_beats); [Licklider et al., FAA/aerospace tech report record](https://www.faa.gov/data_research/research/med_humanfacs/oamtechreports/1960s/1963/196317)

### Inferences
- Headphones are required because a binaural beat needs a different tone in each ear (dichotic presentation). Over speakers the tones mix acoustically into ordinary (monaural) beats. Isochronic tones (a single tone switched on and off) do not need headphones. This is standard psychoacoustics; no single verifying page was retrieved here.
- Implication for an EQ plugin: a static EQ cannot create binaural beats. That would need a stereo oscillator or generator, or a pitch offset between channels. Any "binaural" preset is a different product feature from EQ. If included, it should use carriers under about 1000 Hz and beat rates of 1–30 Hz, and must not claim entrainment or anxiety reduction.
- The g=0.45 figure comes from heterogeneous small studies and does not show a specific frequency-to-state mapping (for example "theta = meditation"). The 2023 review undercuts the claimed mechanism.

### Gaps
- I found no systematic review specific to isochronic tones. There is also no retrieved evidence on safety (for example photic/auditory seizure risk) or on long-term use.

## 6. What IS supported: music and emotion (contrast with frequency-specific claims)

### Takeaway
Well-replicated music-psychology research shows that emotional expression and arousal in music come from *combinations* of cues: tempo, mode (major/minor), loudness or dynamics, pitch register, articulation and timbre (brightness or attack). These are probabilistic cues, not fixed codes, and no single "magic" frequency has been shown to produce a specific emotional or physiological state. Tempo and mode are the strongest cues; loudness tracks arousal. (Confidence: **High** for the general cue model. **Medium** for the relative weights.)

### Cited Findings
- [VERIFIED] Juslin & Laukka (2003) meta-analysed emotion cues in speech and music and is the standard reference for the cue-based model. (High) — [Frontiers in Psychology 2013 (Eerola et al.) citing it](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00487/full); [Oxford Handbook of Music Psychology](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34489/chapter/292614599)
- [VERIFIED] Loud music is perceived as high arousal and soft music as low arousal. (High) — [PMC3726864](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3726864)
- [VERIFIED] Faster tempos are judged happier than slower ones. Major mode is associated with positive emotions and minor mode with negative emotions, especially sadness. (High) — [Corrigall & Schellenberg 2013, Univ. Toronto](https://sites.utm.utoronto.ca/sites/sites.utm.utoronto.ca.glenn_website/files/download/CorrigallSchellenberg2013.pdf); [PMC2683716](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2683716)
- [VERIFIED] Timbre features such as attack and spectral content contribute to perceived emotion. Cues are probabilistic and combined, not deterministic. (Medium) — [Eerola et al. 2013, Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00487/pdf); [Durham repository](https://durham-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1448427)

### Inferences
- What an EQ can honestly do: change spectral balance, which means perceived brightness or darkness, warmth, harshness and clarity. These are timbre cues that plausibly shift how *calm/dark* or *bright/energetic* a mix sounds. It is defensible to name presets for an aesthetic ("Warm / Soft", "Airy") and to say a dark, warm EQ curve "can give a mellower character". It is not defensible to say a 1 kHz or 528 Hz bell "reduces stress".
- Background knowledge, not verified here: Eerola et al. (2013) found mode and tempo carried the most weight among the manipulated cues.

## 7. Legal constraints on marketing (US FTC/FDA, UK ASA/CAP, EU)

### Takeaway
In the US, any objective health claim ("reduces anxiety", "improves sleep", "heals", "repairs DNA") must be backed by "competent and reliable scientific evidence". Under the FTC's December 2022 Health Products Compliance Guidance, that generally means randomised, controlled, (ideally double-blind) human trials on the actual product. The current frequency literature does not meet that bar. The FDA's General Wellness guidance (2016, 2019, reissued January 2026) tolerates low-risk "relax / manage stress" claims for wellness products as long as they never mention disease (including anxiety disorders), but FTC truth-in-advertising rules still apply. In the UK, the ASA says it has yet to see convincing evidence that any healing therapy treats physical or mental conditions and requires robust clinical evidence. No FTC or ASA enforcement action specifically against binaural or "Solfeggio" audio products was found, but closely analogous health-app cases exist. Safe positioning is aesthetic, cultural or historical ("inspired by", "named after", "for creative use", "no health effect is claimed"). (Confidence: **High** on the FTC standard and ASA position. **Medium** on the details of FDA's 2026 reissue. **Medium** that no frequency-specific action exists, since the search may have missed one.)

### Inferences
- Wording risk ladder for the plugin (derived from the FTC, FDA and ASA findings above):
  - **Lowest risk (aesthetic, historical or descriptive):**
    - "Presets inspired by popular 'Solfeggio' and 432 Hz numbers"
    - "Centre frequencies named after…"
    - "For creative and aesthetic sound-shaping"
    - "Warm / bright / airy tonal curves"
    - "No therapeutic or health effects are claimed"
    - "These numbers come from modern numerology, not science"
  - **Moderate risk (implied wellness; needs care and disclaimers, and possibly evidence under FTC rules):**
    - "Relaxing presets"
    - "Create calming mixes"
    - "Meditation-ready sound"
    
    Keep these about the *sound's character* or the user's creative intent ("design a calm-sounding mix"), not about effects on the listener's body.
  - **High risk (objective health claims that need RCT-level substantiation and would breach ASA/CAP without it):**
    - "Reduces anxiety / stress / cortisol"
    - "Improves sleep"
    - "Heals"
    - "Repairs DNA"
    - "Raises vibration / balances chakras" when presented as physical effect
    - "Entrains brainwaves"
    - "Clinically proven"
    - "Scientifically tuned to the Earth's frequency"
    - Citing Akimoto or Calamassi as proof
  - **Also risky:** testimonials implying health outcomes, since endorsements are treated as claims by the advertiser. Disease words such as "anxiety disorder", "insomnia", "depression", "tinnitus" or "ADHD" would also take the product outside FDA's wellness safe harbour.
- An audio EQ plugin marketed for music production is not a medical device. FDA exposure only arises if the plugin is marketed for health purposes. The practical risk is FTC (US) and ASA/CAP and consumer-protection law (UK/EU) for misleading claims, plus app-store and platform policies.
- An "it's traditional or spiritual" disclaimer does not cure an express health claim (see the FTC herbal-tea example). Avoid making the claim at all rather than relying on disclaimers.
- Background knowledge, not verified in this session: the FTC's 2016 Lumosity "brain training" settlement ($2M) is the closest well-known analogue for cognitive and mood claims about consumer software. EU rules flow from the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (2005/29/EC), which bans misleading claims. Its Annex I blacklist includes falsely claiming a product can cure illnesses. In the UK, the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 gave the CMA direct fining powers for unfair practices.
